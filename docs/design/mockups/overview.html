Classic Touch UI proposals: pick one of three

Two screens each: the home page (choose a system) on the left, the running emulator screen on the right. The device bodies are all simplified shapes we draw ourselves as vectors, with no Apple logo, product images or trademark wording. The guest screens are screenshots we ran ourselves.

Proposal A
Timeline

Black text on white, like a yearbook: large condensed years (the system's built-in DIN Condensed), thin rules, line-drawn device bodies, with red marking only "running" and "experimental". Years not imported are gray, and iOS 8 to 10 have a spot marked "Later". The emulator screen switches to a black background, with a large year at the top and three large square buttons below.

Strengths
The sense of time comes from typography rather than effects, clean and with character; it does not imitate any generation of Apple's interface.
Guest screen
360 × 540 pt (1.125x the original device)
Effort
Medium to small, about 600 lines. The home page is a restyled ordinary table, plus line-drawn device bodies.
Watch out
Years are counted by the first release year of the major version (3.1.3 counts as 2009); not-imported and "Later" years are only hints, and tapping them shows an explanation.

Proposal B
Nostalgic skeuomorphic

A tribute to the textures before iOS 6: blue-gray gradient navigation bar, vertical pinstripe background, white rounded grouped tables, glossy glass buttons. The emulator screen puts the guest screen inside a device body we draw ourselves, shown 1:1 with the original device; tapping the round button on the body is Home, the top button is Power, and each model gets its own body.

Strengths
Longtime players get a "we're back" feeling as soon as they open it, and the App feels of a piece with the old systems inside it.
Guest screen
320 × 480 pt (1:1 with the original device), about 20% smaller than the current full-width layout
Effort
The largest. Textures, gloss and device bodies are all drawn in code, at least 4 device bodies, about 1000 lines or more.
Watch out
Rough textures and gloss look cheap instead, so the details need several rounds of tuning.

Proposal C
Modern cards

The same approach as current iOS: a large title, large horizontally swiping model cards (the major version number, with the device illustration showing this system's last screen before shutdown), and the one that is running sits at the bottom like a music player. The emulator screen is all black, the guest screen fills the full width, and a glass capsule at the bottom holds Home and Power.

Strengths
Clean and consistent with the system's built-in Apps; the screen is the largest and clearest, and it is the fastest to finish.
Guest screen
402 × 603 pt (full width, 1.26x the original device, same as now)
Effort
The smallest, about 500 lines. iOS 26 and later use the system glass material; 16 to 25 fall back to frosted glass.
Watch out
The "last screen" in a card only exists once that system has been booted once (the App cannot ship screenshots of Apple's systems); before that it shows a placeholder.
